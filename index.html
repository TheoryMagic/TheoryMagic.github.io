I study the mathematical foundations of learning and decision-making under uncertainty.

I am a Ph.D. student at Oregon State University, advised by Huazheng Wang. My research focuses on reinforcement learning theory, bandit algorithms, and statistical inference. I am interested in how learning algorithms behave when models are imperfect, rewards are unreliable, or data are collected adaptively.

I received my B.S. in Statistics from the University of Science and Technology of China (USTC), in the School of the Gifted Young. From July to October 2026, I was a research intern at Microsoft Research Asia, working with Wei Chen on reward hacking and adaptive reward proxy refinement.

I am seeking Summer 2027 research internships in reinforcement learning, learning theory, and reliable agent evaluation. Please get in touch if our research interests overlap.

Research

My work connects algorithm design with statistical analysis. Three questions guide my research.

  • How do modeling assumptions affect reinforcement learning? I study KL regularization and function approximation, with a focus on what changes when the model class cannot fully represent the environment.
  • When does optimizing a reward lead to the wrong outcome? I study reward poisoning and reward hacking, including how additional feedback can help identify and refine unreliable reward proxies.
  • How should a learner collect information? I study bandit problems that balance decision quality, evaluation cost, and statistical inference, including improving proxies and network interference.

Research Experience

Microsoft Research Asia (MSRA)

July–October 2026

Research Intern · Mentor: Wei Chen

My internship explored a practical question in reward hacking. How can occasional, costly feedback improve a cheap reward proxy, and when should the system request further evaluation?

  • Investigated extensions of existing reward hacking theory to understand how optimizing a proxy reward affects the true reward and how additional feedback can guide proxy refinement.
  • Studied adaptive methods for refining reward proxies using feedback from more capable LLMs, human supervision, or comprehensive environment evaluations. The aim was to reduce reliance on costly feedback as training progresses, while flagging potential proxy failures for further evaluation.
  • Conducted preliminary experiments with coding agents and robotics tasks to examine proxy refinement, discrepancies between proxy and true rewards, and evaluation cost.
